## 1. Abstract — Revised Version

The abstract must shift from "RL learns event-triggered dynamic control" to "prediction-driven framework reveals regime conditions for adaptive scheduling value." Replace the entire abstract with:

```latex
\begin{abstract}
Antarctic automatic weather stations (AWS) must balance measurement quality against
tight power budgets when scheduling heterogeneous sensors that include high-cost
instruments with non-negligible warm-up delays.
We propose a \emph{prediction-driven proximal policy optimization} (PD-PPO) framework
that integrates a multi-horizon forecast oracle into the scheduling agent's observation
space, enabling the policy to anticipate blowing-snow events and manage warm-up costs
proactively.
On a fixed-budget benchmark (B\,=\,1.70) with five independent seeds, PD-PPO reduces
forecast-weighted mean absolute error (FW-MAE) by 3.2\% relative to round-robin
scheduling and by 12.1\% relative to age-of-information (AoI) scheduling, while
approaching the performance of the best feasible static sensor subset (1.5\% gap).
Behavior diagnostics reveal that under this budget level the energy constraint is
non-binding at the optimal fixed subset, consistent with the theoretical prediction
that the optimal policy degenerates to a fixed-subset selection when the constraint is
inactive~\citep{FernandezBes2015}.
A complementary energy-account diagnostic (harvest\,=\,0.92, capacity\,=\,180,
B\,=\,1.20) demonstrates that when the constraint is genuinely active, a dynamic
snow-core-to-event-laser strategy achieves a 1.9\% FW-MAE improvement over the best
static subset in storm-window evaluation (event fraction 42--70\%), with a 9.3\%
advantage in event-period loss.
These results establish regime-dependent conditions under which adaptive scheduling
provides quantifiable value beyond static baselines, and motivate energy-account-aware
RL training as a direction for future work.
\end{abstract}
```

\paragraph{Deployment note on event detection.}
In the simulation, the event flag $z_t$ is derived from the ground-truth blowing-snow

### §6.4 Energy-Account Diagnostic (NEW SECTION)

This is the most important new section. Add it as a new subsection:

```latex
\subsection{Energy-Account Diagnostic: Regime Conditions for Adaptive Scheduling}
\label{sec:energy_account}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
The fixed-budget benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:fixed_budget}) reveals that PD-PPO
adopts a quasi-static strategy under $B = 1.70$, consistent with the non-binding
constraint regime.  To identify the conditions under which dynamic scheduling
provides genuine value, we introduce an \emph{energy-account} model that
distinguishes instantaneous feasibility ($\text{power} < B$) from long-run
sustainability ($\text{power} < h$, where $h$ is the harvest rate).

\paragraph{Physical calibration.}
We set $B = 1.20$, $h = 0.92$, battery capacity $= 180$, and reserve $= 20$
(all in normalized units).  The harvest rate $h = 0.92$ is calibrated to the
long-run average power consumption of the dynamic snow-core-to-event-laser
strategy: $0.87 \times 0.73 + 1.21 \times 0.27 \approx 0.96$, with $h$ set
slightly below this value to ensure the constraint is active.
The capacity of 180 provides a buffer for the four-step laser warm-up
($4 \times (1.25 - 0.92) = 1.32$ normalized units) with adequate safety margin.

\paragraph{Constraint activation mechanism.}
Under this model, the static laser-always-on subset
(met + surface + laser, power $= 1.14 < B = 1.20$, instantaneously feasible)
becomes long-run unsustainable: each step incurs a SOC deficit of
$1.14 - 0.92 = 0.22$ units, exhausting the battery over a storm window.
In storm-window evaluation (six high-event-density windows, event fraction
42--70\%), the SOC guard triggers 438 times for the static laser subset,
effectively disabling it.  By contrast, the dynamic snow-core-to-event-laser
strategy (long-run average power $\approx 0.92$) maintains $\text{SOC}_{\min}
= 48.48$ with zero guard violations and zero warm-up aborts.

\paragraph{Oracle-level results.}
Table~\ref{tab:energy_account} reports storm-window and full-distribution
evaluation results for the energy-account curriculum PPO (100k steps,
seeds 41--45) alongside oracle and static baselines.

% --- INSERT TABLE HERE (see Table 5 specification below) ---

In storm-window evaluation, PD-PPO achieves $0.4153 \pm 0.0051$, beating
AoI ($0.4176 \pm 0.0105$, 3/5 seeds) and round-robin ($0.4451 \pm 0.0167$,
5/5 seeds), while remaining below the full-observation upper bound
($0.3961 \pm 0.0021$).  The oracle dynamic strategy
(snow-core $\to$ event-laser) achieves $0.4169$ in storm-window evaluation,
demonstrating a 1.9\% improvement over the best static subset
(snow-core static, $0.4248$), with a 9.3\% event-period advantage
($0.3190$ vs.\ $0.3517$).

In full-distribution no-retrain evaluation, PD-PPO achieves
$0.3155 \pm 0.0133$, with 4/5 seeds beating AoI ($0.3168 \pm 0.0135$)
and 4/5 seeds beating static projection ($0.3318 \pm 0.0062$).
This demonstrates that the storm-window curriculum does not overfit:
the learned policy generalizes to the full distribution.

\paragraph{Mechanism diagnosis.}
A three-seed mechanism analysis reveals that PD-PPO's advantage over AoI
in storm-window evaluation is driven primarily by non-event-period loss
($0.5256$ vs.\ $0.5431$) rather than event-period loss ($0.3274$ vs.\
$0.3243$).  The laser event-to-non-event activation ratio is approximately
$1.03\times$, indicating that PD-PPO has not learned clean event-triggered
laser gating.  Instead, the policy learns conservative storm-window energy
management: maintaining a compact sensor subset that avoids SOC depletion
while preserving measurement quality across both event and non-event periods.
This is consistent with the structural diagnosis that single-step PPO
gradients cannot simultaneously optimize two time-scale behaviors
(short-horizon event activation and long-horizon SOC reserve management).

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
These results establish that the energy-account regime ($B = 1.20$,
$h = 0.92$) is the correct setting for evaluating adaptive scheduling value.
The storm-window oracle advantage ($\Delta = 0.0079$) demonstrates that
prediction-driven warm-up has quantifiable value when the constraint is
genuinely active.  Whether a trained RL agent can fully exploit this
advantage through learned event-conditional gating remains an open question
and is identified as a primary direction for future work
(Section~\ref{sec:future_work}).
```

**New text:**

```latex
\subsection{Regime-Dependent Value of Adaptive Scheduling}
\label{sec:regime_dependent}

The experimental results reveal a clear regime structure governing the value of
adaptive scheduling.  Under the fixed-budget benchmark ($B = 1.70$), PD-PPO
converges to a quasi-static compact-subset strategy, approaching but not
surpassing the best feasible static projection.  This behavior is not a
shortcoming of the RL training but rather the theoretically correct response
to the budget regime: \citet{FernandezBes2015} (Theorem~1) prove that when
the energy constraint is inactive at the optimal fixed subset, the optimal
stationary policy is a constant threshold (fixed-subset selection).
Under $B = 1.70$, the optimal fixed subset consumes $1.46$ normalized units,
leaving a margin of $0.24$ that renders the constraint non-binding.

The energy-account diagnostic (Section~\ref{sec:energy_account}) identifies
the complementary regime: when $B = 1.20$ and harvest $h = 0.92$, the
constraint is genuinely active, and the static laser-always-on strategy
becomes long-run unsustainable.  In this regime, the oracle dynamic strategy
achieves a measurable storm-window advantage ($\Delta = 0.0079$, 1.9\%),
with a substantially larger event-period advantage ($\Delta = 0.0327$, 9.3\%).

These two regimes together provide a principled answer to the question of
when adaptive scheduling is worth the complexity cost: adaptive scheduling
provides value when (i) the energy constraint is active (harvest rate below
the power consumption of the best static subset), and (ii) the evaluation
window has sufficient event density to realize the event-period advantage.
Under the fixed-budget benchmark, neither condition is met; under the
energy-account storm-window setting, both are met.

The PD-PPO framework is designed to operate in both regimes: in the
non-binding regime it correctly learns a near-static policy, and in the
binding regime it provides the infrastructure (forecast oracle, warm-up
modeling, curriculum training) needed to exploit dynamic scheduling value.
The present results demonstrate the framework's correctness in the
non-binding regime and establish the oracle-level upper bound for the
binding regime; training a PPO agent to fully exploit the binding-regime
advantage is identified as the primary direction for future work.
```

### §7.4 Limitations (EXPANDED)

Replace or expand the limitations section:

```latex
\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Fixed power budget simplification.}
The primary fixed-budget experiments adopt a constant per-step budget $B$,
which does not model battery SOC dynamics or harvest fluctuations.
The energy-account extension (Section~\ref{sec:energy_account}) addresses
this limitation for the diagnostic experiments, but the main PPO training
loop does not yet incorporate SOC as an observation.  Future work should
integrate the energy-account model into the RL training environment,
enabling the agent to learn SOC-aware scheduling policies.

\paragraph{Event flag information assumption.}
The event flag $z_t$ used in the observation space is derived from the
simulation ground truth.  In real AWS deployment, this signal would need
to be estimated from always-on low-cost sensors (e.g., wind speed threshold
from the GMX500 MaxiMet), introducing estimation uncertainty.
The present results represent an optimistic information assumption;
deployment performance may degrade if the event probability estimate
is noisy or delayed.

\paragraph{Single-step gradient limitation.}
The mechanism diagnosis (Section~\ref{sec:energy_account}) reveals that
PD-PPO has not learned clean event-triggered laser gating in the
energy-account setting.  The laser event-to-non-event activation ratio
of $\approx 1.03\times$ indicates that the policy manages energy
conservatively across both event and non-event periods rather than
concentrating laser activation on events.  This is consistent with the
structural limitation that single-step PPO gradients cannot simultaneously
optimize short-horizon event activation and long-horizon SOC reserve
management.  Hierarchical RL or multi-timescale critics may be needed
to learn clean event-conditional gating.

\paragraph{SOC soft penalty as future direction.}
A single-seed probe of a SOC soft penalty formulation (storm-window loss
$0.4069$ vs.\ AoI $0.4128$, abort rate $= 9$) suggests that explicit
SOC regularization may improve energy management without sacrificing
measurement quality.  This direction was not scaled to $n = 5$ seeds
in the present work and is identified as a promising avenue for future
investigation.

\paragraph{Synthetic data calibration.}
The synthetic time series are calibrated to multi-year Antarctic AWS
statistics from the AntAWS dataset~\citep{AntAWS2023} using DFT phase
randomization, which preserves statistical properties (mean, variance,
autocorrelation structure) but does not model seasonal trends or
polar-day/polar-night transitions.  Validation on real multi-year AWS
records with explicit seasonal structure is left for future work.
```
