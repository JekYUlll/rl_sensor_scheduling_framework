```latex
\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:non_equivalence}}
\label{apx:proof_prop2}

Set the common objective horizon to $T=1$; then all three objectives assign the
same temporal weight $\gamma^0 = 1$ to the evaluable step. Consider one forecast
target, one forecast horizon, and two independent scalar Gaussian random walks
\begin{equation}
  z_{t+1} = z_t + \xi_t,
  \qquad
  e_{t+1} = e_t + \zeta_t,
  \label{eq:prop2_rw}
\end{equation}
where $\xi_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,q_z)$ and $\zeta_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,q_e)$ are
mutually independent over time. The one-step forecast target is
\begin{equation}
  y_{t+1} = c z_t + M e_t + \eta_t,
  \label{eq:prop2_target}
\end{equation}
with independent $\eta_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_\eta^2)$ and coefficients
$M > c > 0$.

Suppose that one random-walk component can be measured without noise at each
time step. At an evaluable time step, feasible schedules can produce age vectors
$d^{(1)}=(0,1)$ under policy $\pi_1$ and $d^{(2)}=(2,0)$ under policy $\pi_2$.
These vectors can arise from feasible one-measurement schedules that prioritize
different random-walk components before the evaluable time step. Thus $\pi_1$
has lower total AoI because $1 < 2$.

The corresponding posterior error covariances are
\begin{equation}
  P^{(1)} = \mathrm{diag}(0,q_e),
  \qquad
  P^{(2)} = \mathrm{diag}(2q_z,0).
\end{equation}
Choose $q_z > 0$ and
\begin{equation}
  \frac{2c^2 q_z}{M^2} < q_e < 2q_z.
  \label{eq:prop2_variance_order}
\end{equation}
This is possible because $M > c$. The right inequality gives
$\mathrm{tr}(P^{(1)}) < \mathrm{tr}(P^{(2)})$, so the covariance-trace objective
also prefers $\pi_1$.

Now consider the forecast objective in \Cref{eq:prop2_fcst_objective}. Take the
single-target, single-horizon, positive-weight special case of
\Cref{eq:step_forecast_loss}; then the denominator in \Cref{eq:step_forecast_loss}
is a positive constant and the per-step loss reduces to
\begin{equation}
  L_{\mathrm{step}}(t)
  =
  \min\!\left\{
    10,\,
    \frac{\left| \hat y_{t+1} - y_{t+1} \right|}{\sigma}
  \right\},
  \label{eq:prop2_single_target_loss}
\end{equation}
for some target scale $\sigma > 0$.

Under $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$, the one-step forecast errors are zero-mean Gaussian
with variances
\begin{equation}
  v_1 = M^2 q_e + \sigma_\eta^2,
  \qquad
  v_2 = 2c^2 q_z + \sigma_\eta^2.
  \label{eq:prop2_error_variances}
\end{equation}
The left inequality in \Cref{eq:prop2_variance_order} gives $v_1 > v_2$.

For $v > 0$, let $Z_v \sim \mathcal{N}(0,v)$ and define
\begin{equation}
  g(v)
  =
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[
    \min\!\left\{10,\frac{|Z_v|}{\sigma}\right\}
  \right].
\end{equation}
Write $Z_v = \sqrt{v}\,U$ with $U \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$. If $v_1 > v_2$, then
for every $u$,
\begin{equation}
  \min\!\left\{10,\frac{\sqrt{v_1}|u|}{\sigma}\right\}
  \ge
  \min\!\left\{10,\frac{\sqrt{v_2}|u|}{\sigma}\right\},
\end{equation}
and the inequality is strict on the set
\begin{equation}
  0 < |u| < \frac{10\sigma}{\sqrt{v_1}},
\end{equation}
which has positive probability under $U \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$. Therefore
$g(v_1) > g(v_2)$.

Applying this to \Cref{eq:prop2_error_variances} yields
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[L_{\mathrm{step}}(t;\pi_1)]
  >
  \mathbb{E}[L_{\mathrm{step}}(t;\pi_2)].
\end{equation}
Because $T=1$, this implies
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{fcst}}(\pi_2) > J_{\mathrm{fcst}}(\pi_1).
\end{equation}

Hence the AoI and covariance objectives prefer $\pi_1$, while the forecast-loss
objective prefers $\pi_2$, proving that the three objectives can rank feasible
policies differently.
```

```latex
Forecast loss, AoI, and covariance trace evaluate different properties of a sensor
schedule. Let $\pi$ be a feasible scheduling policy and let $\gamma \in (0,1]$
denote a discount factor. For the theoretical non-equivalence statement below,
define the discounted absolute forecast objective
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{abs}}(\pi)
  =
  -\mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[
    \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^t
    \left|\hat y_{t+1}(\pi)-y_{t+1}\right|
  \right].
  \label{eq:theoretical_abs_forecast_objective}
\end{equation}
This theoretical objective is used only in Proposition~\ref{prop:non_equivalence_abs}.
It is distinct from the capped, target-weighted, scale-normalized empirical loss
$L_{\mathrm{step}}$ in \Cref{eq:step_forecast_loss}, which is used elsewhere in the
manuscript for policy training and evaluation.

For a separate model-based estimator with posterior covariance $P_t$, the AoI and
covariance objectives are
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{AoI}}(\pi)
  =
  -\mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[
    \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^t \sum_{j\in\mathcal{Y}} \mathrm{age}_{t,j}
  \right],
  \qquad
  J_{\mathrm{cov}}(\pi)
  =
  -\mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[
    \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^t \,\mathrm{tr}(P_t)
  \right].
\end{equation}

Proposition 2 (Non-equivalence of a theoretical forecast-loss objective, AoI, and
covariance objectives). A theoretical absolute forecast-loss objective, covariance
trace, and per-variable AoI objectives can induce different orderings over feasible
sensor schedules. In particular, there exist a common horizon $T$, a common
discount factor $\gamma$, and two feasible policies $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ such that
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{AoI}}(\pi_1) > J_{\mathrm{AoI}}(\pi_2),
  \qquad
  J_{\mathrm{cov}}(\pi_1) > J_{\mathrm{cov}}(\pi_2),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{abs}}(\pi_2) > J_{\mathrm{abs}}(\pi_1).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $\pi_1$ has lower AoI and lower covariance trace, and $\pi_2$ has
strictly lower theoretical absolute forecast loss.
```

\caption{PD-PPO policy training}
\label{alg:pdppo_training}
\begin{algorithmic}

\State Evaluate the candidate fixed channel subsets on the validation partition and compute $s_{\mathrm{calm}}$, the event-type-specific normalization factors $s_c$, and the condition-specific supervised target actions.
