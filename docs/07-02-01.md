## What the new evidence now establishes

Based on the supplementary summary you provided, the new evidence changes the paper in a structured way.

The **forecast-greedy one-step baseline** is an important win for the paper. A clean 24/24 advantage with large positive mean step and macro margins directly supports the claim that PD-PPO is doing something sequential and non-myopic, rather than merely approximating a one-step forecast-loss argmin. That result is especially valuable because the greedy baseline is strong in a way reviewers intuitively understand: it already has direct access to the evaluation target at the immediate step. In presentation terms, this is now one of your most persuasive pieces of evidence.

The **context-alert bandit** is the main constraint on the manuscript narrative. Since it beats PD-PPO on step loss in 17/24 seeds and macro score in most seeds, you can no longer claim that RL is broadly superior to context-aware hand-crafted decision rules. This result does not erase the value of PD-PPO; it means the present implementation is **not yet the strongest adaptive decision maker in this benchmark family** when compared with a carefully engineered context policy. That should be treated as a substantive scientific result, not buried as embarrassment.

The **AoI reward proxy** helps, but only modestly. With only two seeds, it supports an exploratory statement that rewarding downstream forecast loss may be more aligned with the final objective than an AoI-style proxy in this setting. It does **not** support a final headline claim of reward superiority.

The **coverage reward proxy** is the most important caution for the reward story. Since it is competitive on the same two-seed slice, the data no longer support any strong statement that “forecast reward alone explains the gain.” The observed performance appears to depend on **the interaction** among reward design, action geometry, auxiliary supervision, and scenario structure. That interpretation also fits the current manuscript, which already presents the policy as a full masked-PPO system with auxiliary components rather than a plain reward swap. fileciteturn0file0

## Which claims to keep, soften, and remove

The manuscript should now operate with a deliberately calibrated claim hierarchy.

### Claims you can keep

You can still claim that PD-PPO is a **prediction-driven constrained RL scheduling framework** for a power-limited specialist-allocation problem. That statement is directly aligned with the current method section, where the policy acts over feasible candidate masks under power, startup, and minimum-duration rules and is trained with forecast loss from a fixed forecaster. fileciteturn0file0

You can also claim that PD-PPO **outperforms strong fixed-mask references and simple dynamic rules** on the original 24-seed benchmark, and that the learned policy’s gains are associated with state-dependent specialist allocation rather than hidden constant-mask or periodic-rotation shortcuts, because the paper already includes fixed-mask replay and behavioural diagnostics for that purpose. fileciteturn0file0

After the supplement, you can add a new supported claim:

**PD-PPO is not reducible to a one-step myopic forecast-greedy policy.**

This is now much stronger than before and should become a front-row result.

### Claims you should soften

You should soften the reward claim to something like:

**Pilot evidence suggests forecast-loss reward is more aligned with the target task than an AoI-style proxy, but the present reward-ablation evidence is exploratory and mixed.**

That wording is faithful to the two-seed AoI and coverage pilots you reported.

You should also soften the “RL is necessary” narrative. The safer framing is:

**Sequential RL provides value beyond one-step greedy selection, but context-sensitive hand-engineered policies remain competitive and can even outperform the current PD-PPO implementation.**

That is still a respectable methods paper position.

### Claims you should remove

Three claims should disappear from the paper.

The first is any claim of **unconditional superiority over context-aware adaptive policies**. Your supplement directly contradicts that.

The second is any claim that **forecast reward itself is the sole driver of the observed gains**. The coverage proxy result rules that out.

The third is any claim that **PD-PPO, as an RL algorithm, is shown to be stronger than alternative learning paradigms in general**. The paper already did not contain DQN-style RL comparisons, and the supplement does not change that. The journal asks for clear, standard terminology and genuine contribution claims, which argues for precision here rather than inflation. citeturn5view0

## How the manuscript should be rewritten

The most effective repair is not cosmetic editing. It is a targeted rewrite of the manuscript’s claim architecture.

### Abstract

The abstract should stop implying universal adaptive superiority and should instead present a two-part result:

1. PD-PPO beats fixed-mask and one-step forecast-greedy baselines.
2. A strong context-alert bandit remains a competitive or stronger challenge.

A good abstract-level sentence would be:

> Supplementary comparisons show that PD-PPO consistently outperforms one-step forecast-greedy scheduling, supporting the value of sequential non-myopic control, while a hand-engineered context-alert bandit remains a strong challenge and outperforms the current policy on many seeds.

That sentence makes the paper look more mature, not weaker.

The current abstract emphasizes gains over fixed masks and rule-based references and presents replay plus behavioural checks as evidence that the learned policy uses multiple specialists and depends on event context. That core remains useful and should stay. fileciteturn0file0

### Introduction

Your introduction currently asks whether a learned scheduler can outperform a validation-selected fixed specialist and simple dynamic references under a common downstream forecaster. That framing is still valid, but the new supplement means the paper should add one more explicit question:

> Is the gain due to genuinely sequential control, and how does the learned policy compare with a strong context-aware memoryless alternative?

That one sentence turns the supplement into a natural extension of the paper rather than a late-stage patch.

The contributions section should be rewritten along these lines:

- a prediction-driven constrained scheduling formulation under hard operating rules;
- a masked PPO implementation with executable feasibility masking and forecast-loss training;
- an evaluation protocol that distinguishes fixed, myopic, context-aware, and privileged adaptive references.

That third contribution is stronger than the original one because it now includes the new baseline family.

### Results

The biggest change belongs here.

Your current results section states that PD-PPO improves over fixed masks and rule-based dynamic references in all 24 seeds, and the tables are built around that confirmation logic. fileciteturn0file0 Once the context-alert bandit is included, you need to split results into **supporting evidence** and **challenging evidence**.

A very clean subsection order would be:

1. **Main fixed and rule-based comparisons**  
   Keep the original 24-seed result.

2. **One-step forecast-greedy comparison**  
   Present this as the strongest evidence for sequential value.

3. **Context-alert bandit comparison**  
   Present this as the strongest non-RL adaptive challenge.

4. **Reward pilots**  
   Explicitly mark AoI and coverage as pilot checks.

This order lets the reader build belief in the framework before confronting its current weakness, while still remaining honest.

### Discussion

The discussion should now do more real scientific work.

At present, the manuscript says that contextual bandits and threshold policies would be valuable future comparators. Since you now have one, that paragraph must be rewritten. fileciteturn0file0

The discussion should say three things clearly:

First, the one-step greedy result shows that the learned policy is exploiting sequential effects created by operating rules, delayed forecast consequences, or action-history state.

Second, the context-alert bandit shows that much of the benchmark’s gain can be harvested by good context engineering. That means the paper’s value lies more in **general-purpose constrained adaptive scheduling under forecast-based evaluation** than in proving that RL is the universally best controller.

Third, the mixed reward pilots imply that reward choice is only one part of the system. Action-space design, support losses, and benchmark structure also matter.

This discussion actually strengthens the paper’s intellectual honesty.

### Conclusion

The conclusion should end with a bounded takeaway:

> Within the fixed-forecaster benchmark, PD-PPO provides a viable prediction-driven constrained scheduling framework, clearly surpasses fixed and one-step myopic references, and yields interpretable adaptive specialist allocation, while a context-engineered bandit remains a strong challenge for future improvement.

That is a very strong ESWA-style ending because it states achievement, boundary, and next scientific question together. ESWA’s guidance explicitly favors clarity and context-specific explanations over inflated novelty language. citeturn5view0

## The context-alert bandit needs explicit status labeling

This is the most important editorial decision left.

There are two very different ways to present the context-alert bandit, and which one is legitimate depends on its information access.

### If it uses the same online observables and the same feasibility rules

Then it is a **true main baseline** and must appear in the main paper, not only in the supplement. In that case, the paper should state plainly that a hand-engineered context policy currently outperforms PD-PPO on the 24-seed supplement. Hiding it would be risky in review.

This does **not** make the paper unpublishable. It simply changes the contribution from “RL wins” to “framework and protocol clarify where RL helps and where engineering still dominates.”

### If it uses privileged processing, tuned thresholds with leakage, or stronger side information

Then it should be relabeled as a **diagnostic or privileged challenger**, similar in spirit to your current event-label replay diagnostic, and its stronger information access must be explained clearly. The legitimacy of demoting it depends entirely on whether it enjoys information or tuning that PD-PPO does not.

That status distinction should be made with zero ambiguity in the paper.
