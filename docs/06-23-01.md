# Revising Experimental Terminology and Evidence Presentation for Expert Systems with Applications

## Executive summary

The manuscript already contains a publishable applied-systems core: a PPO-based scheduler chooses admissible sensor subsets under a constrained action geometry, and performance is judged by downstream forecast error under a chronologically separated train/validation/test design. The main revision need is therefore not to rename the method, and not to remove **PD-PPO** as the high-level method label, but to replace evaluation-specific jargon with standard experimental language, reduce binary “gate” rhetoric, and make metric definitions and statistical claims directly auditable. fileciteturn0file0

This matters especially for *Expert Systems with Applications* because the journal explicitly asks authors to use **standard optimization terminology**, to explain components formally, and to avoid superficial renaming of existing ideas. Its guide for authors also expects clearly structured sections, definitions for field-specific terms, editable mathematics and tables, and transparent data-availability statements. In other words, ESWA’s editorial standard aligns with a revision that keeps the method name but rewrites the experimental layer into conventional terminology such as *primary metric*, *secondary metric*, *diagnostic baseline*, *behavioral diagnostic*, *paired comparison*, *confidence interval*, and *sensitivity analysis*. citeturn3view0turn4view0turn4view2turn4view4

The most consequential experimental problems are concentrated in four places. First, the **static-normalised event-regime macro score** is a ratio metric whose denominator is derived from a baseline family chosen on validation, which makes the headline score partly endogenous to the benchmark construction. Second, the **gate** language collapses continuous evidence into pass/fail outcomes and gives binary thresholds more prominence than effect size and uncertainty. Third, the **behaviour-complexity gate** mixes descriptive diagnostics with confirmatory evidence while leaving thresholds and exact operationalization partly unspecified in the manuscript text available here. Fourth, the **24/24 seeds** framing and sign-test emphasis are too thin for an RL paper, because current RL evaluation literature strongly recommends interval estimates and fuller treatment of run-to-run uncertainty rather than relying mainly on point estimates or all-pass counts. fileciteturn0file0 citeturn8view0turn8view1turn17view0turn18view1turn18view2

The revision path is straightforward. Keep **PD-PPO**. Replace “projection layer” with **feasibility projection** or **constraint-enforcing action projection**. Replace all “gates” with ordinary statistical comparison terminology. Recast “replay diagnostics” as **event-aware upper-bound baselines** or **privileged-information diagnostic baselines**. Recast the behavior section as **behavioral diagnostics** rather than a pass/fail certification. Make the primary result a conventional regime-balanced test loss, with standardized scaling based on training-only quantities if scaling is needed at all. Then report seed-level paired differences, 95% confidence intervals, effect sizes, and a paired nonparametric test, treating the sign test only as supporting evidence. fileciteturn0file0 citeturn3view0turn8view0turn16view0turn18view1

The recommendations below are written so they can be incorporated directly into the revision. Where the current manuscript text leaves a threshold, exact formula, or implementation detail unspecified, I mark it explicitly as **unspecified in manuscript** rather than guessing a hidden definition. fileciteturn0file0

## ESWA fit and revision principles

ESWA’s current author guidance is unusually relevant to this manuscript because it explicitly discourages papers that mainly **rename existing concepts** and urges authors to present methods with **clarity**, **formal grounding**, and **standard optimization terminology**. That language does not forbid a method name such as PD-PPO, but it does push strongly against an evaluation vocabulary that turns standard comparisons into bespoke labels without adding mathematical precision. citeturn3view0

The same guide also asks for clearly structured sections, numbered equations, a glossary for field-specific terms, editable tables, and a transparent data statement. That combination strongly favors a revision in which experimental claims are organized under conventional subsection titles such as *Data split*, *Baselines*, *Primary outcome*, *Secondary outcomes*, *Behavioral diagnostics*, *Statistical analysis*, and *Sensitivity analyses*, with a short glossary containing only the genuinely necessary retained terms. citeturn4view0turn4view1turn4view2turn4view3turn4view4

A second ESWA-relevant consideration is transparency around AI-assisted writing. The journal requires disclosure when generative AI tools are used in manuscript preparation and states that the authors remain fully responsible for accuracy, completeness, and impartiality. Since the manuscript already includes a declaration that Codex was used for code inspection, consistency checks, and language editing, the revision should keep that disclosure while tightening all experiment-facing language so the text clearly reflects the authors’ own analytical judgment rather than tool-generated terminology drift. fileciteturn0file0 citeturn5view0

For this journal, the right editorial posture is therefore: **retain the high-level method name, minimize internal experiment jargon, define every metric once in math, identify one primary outcome, report uncertainty and effect sizes, and move any privileged-information or behavior-check constructs into explicitly labeled supplementary diagnostics.** That approach is fully consistent with ESWA’s stated emphasis on original applied systems work, formal clarity, and transparent reporting. citeturn3view0turn4view0turn4view4

## Metric redesign and statistical reporting

The current manuscript contains several mathematically usable ingredients, but the inferential structure needs to be rewritten from “gate passing” into standard estimation and comparison. Statistical guidance from Demšar, the ASA statement on p-values, and more recent RL evaluation papers points in the same direction: use paired comparisons, report effect sizes and uncertainty intervals, and avoid reducing the scientific conclusion to whether a binary threshold was crossed. citeturn8view0turn8view1turn17view0turn18view1turn18view2

### Static-normalised event-regime macro score

The manuscript defines the ordinary test loss and then the regime-level losses
\[
L_c(\pi)=\frac{1}{|T_c|}\sum_{t\in T_c} L_{\text{step}}(t;\pi),
\]
followed by the headline metric
\[
M_{\text{staticnorm}}(\pi)=\frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{c\in C}\frac{L_c(\pi)}{s_c},
\]
where \(s_c\) is the median regime loss across feasible fixed-static masks on the static-selection partition. That is a precise ratio metric, but its denominator is induced by the fixed-mask candidate family and by a validation-stage design choice rather than by a policy-independent scale. fileciteturn0file0

The statistical problem is not that normalization is always inappropriate. The problem is that this particular normalization is **benchmark-endogenous**. If the candidate mask set changes, if the validation regime mix shifts, or if one regime is easy for all fixed masks, then the denominator changes, and the headline metric changes with it. Ratio metrics also become unstable when denominators are small, and they can obscure whether the claimed gain comes from one regime, all regimes, or from denominator behavior. For a journal review, that creates avoidable argument over whether the score reflects policy quality or construction of the static reference family. fileciteturn0file0 citeturn8view1turn18view3

The clean replacement is:

\[
M_{\text{macro}}(\pi)=\sum_{c\in C}\rho_c L_c(\pi), \qquad \sum_c \rho_c = 1,
\]
with \(\rho_c\) fixed *a priori* and typically chosen as equal weights if the scientific claim is “balanced regime performance.” If standardization is required for comparability across regimes, use an **exogenous regime scale**
\[
M_{\text{std}}(\pi)=\sum_{c\in C}\rho_c \frac{L_c(\pi)}{\tau_c},
\]
where \(\tau_c\) is estimated from training-only quantities, such as the loss of a naive forecaster, target standard deviations, or a forecaster-fit split reference—not from the selected static baseline family. This preserves interpretability and removes endogeneity from the primary metric. citeturn8view1turn16view0

For reporting, I recommend the following hierarchy. The **primary endpoint** should be \(M_{\text{macro}}\) or \(M_{\text{std}}\) on the held-out test set. The **secondary endpoint** should be the overall test loss \(L_{\text{step}}\). In the main results table, report all per-regime losses \(L_c\), the macro average, the overall loss, and the paired difference versus the selected fixed-mask baseline. Then for each metric report a 95% bootstrap confidence interval for the paired mean or median difference across seeds, plus a paired nonparametric test. Plain percentile bootstrap intervals are weaker in small samples; bootstrap methods with better small-sample behavior are preferable. citeturn8view0turn16view0turn18view1

### Replay gates and dynamic headroom

In the current manuscript, “explicit replay gates” appear to formalize the claim that an event-aware replay policy can outperform any single fixed specialist, thereby demonstrating “dynamic headroom.” Mathematically, this can be written as a difference
\[
\Delta_{\text{headroom},s}=M_s(\pi_{\text{static}}^\*)-M_s(\pi_{\text{event-aware}})
\]
for each seed \(s\), where \(\pi_{\text{static}}^\*\) is the selected fixed-mask baseline and \(\pi_{\text{event-aware}}\) chooses actions from event labels or equivalent privileged annotations. fileciteturn0file0

That comparison is useful, but the label **gate** is misleading for two reasons. First, it suggests confirmatory evidence about the learned method when the comparison is actually a *scenario diagnostic* or *upper-bound diagnostic*. Second, if the replay policy uses event-type labels that are unavailable to the learner at deployment, then it is not a baseline in the conventional sense; it is a privileged-information reference. That is informative, but it should be described as such. fileciteturn0file0

The recommended rewrite is to move this construct into a subsection called **Event-aware diagnostic upper bound** or **Privileged-information diagnostic baseline**. Keep the analysis, but state its role narrowly: it quantifies whether the benchmark contains meaningful improvement potential for state- or regime-aware scheduling. Report the seed-level paired difference \(\Delta_{\text{headroom},s}\), its mean or median, a 95% CI, and the win rate. Do not make this comparison part of a multi-gate claim chain. This makes the paper easier to defend, because reviewers can accept the diagnostic without feeling that privileged information has been smuggled into the main proof-of-method narrative. citeturn8view1turn17view0turn18view1

### Behaviour-complexity gate

The manuscript’s behavior section refers to mask entropy, event-mask mutual information, event-sensor \(L_1\) separation, and “not fixed-like / not simple-cycle-like,” but in the manuscript text provided here the exact formal thresholds and pass/fail decision rules are not fully specified. The natural mathematical forms are descriptive statistics such as
\[
H(A)=-\sum_a \hat p(a)\log \hat p(a),
\]
\[
I(C;A)=\sum_{c,a}\hat p(c,a)\log\frac{\hat p(c,a)}{\hat p(c)\hat p(a)},
\]
and regime-conditioned distribution distances such as
\[
D_{L_1}(c,c')=\sum_a \left|\hat p(a\mid c)-\hat p(a\mid c')\right|.
\]
Those are valid descriptive measures. The problem arises when they are fused into a binary gate and then treated as part of the main statistical proof. fileciteturn0file0

There are three validity issues. The first is **threshold opacity**: without fully specified thresholds and decision logic, the audit is not reproducible. The second is **sample-size sensitivity**: entropy and mutual information estimates vary with test-sequence length and discretization. The third is **role confusion**: these metrics describe policy behavior, but they are not primary evidence that the policy is superior in task performance. A policy can be behaviorally diverse and still not materially outperform a fixed baseline, and vice versa. fileciteturn0file0

The best revision is to rename this whole component **Behavioral diagnostics** and remove pass/fail phrasing. Report the metrics descriptively, with uncertainty. For example: action-mask entropy \(H(A)\), switching rate, probability of repeating the previous action, regime–action mutual information \(I(C;A)\), and a non-periodicity diagnostic such as the maximum action autocorrelation over relevant lags or the predictive gain of state features over phase-only features. For statistical analysis, use seed-level summaries with bootstrap CIs, and if independence of regime and action is of interest, use a permutation test within each seed and summarize the resulting effect sizes across seeds. That turns behavior from a brittle certification device into a transparent supporting analysis. citeturn16view0turn18view3

### All-pass seed reporting and sign tests

The manuscript repeatedly emphasizes that several outcomes hold in **24/24 seeds**, and reports one-sided sign-test \(p\)-values for these all-pass results. If the seed-level comparison for seed \(s\) is
\[
d_s = M_s(\pi_{\text{baseline}})-M_s(\pi_{\text{PD-PPO}}),
\]
then “24/24” corresponds to \(\sum_s \mathbf{1}[d_s>0]=24\). A one-sided sign test can absolutely be used on these signs. Demšar also supports nonparametric paired testing in classifier comparisons, and the sign test is mathematically legitimate as a direction-of-effect check. The problem is that it is far too coarse to stand near the center of the paper’s reporting. fileciteturn0file0 citeturn8view0

The ASA statement is directly relevant here: scientific conclusions should not be based only on whether a \(p\)-value clears a threshold, and a \(p\)-value does not measure effect size or practical importance. RL evaluation papers make the same point in a more field-specific way: point estimates and binary summaries can hide large uncertainty, and current best practice is to report interval estimates and fuller characterization of variability across runs. citeturn8view1turn17view0turn18view1turn18view2

The recommended reporting template is:

\[
d_s = M_s(\pi_{\text{baseline}})-M_s(\pi_{\text{PD-PPO}}),
\]
\[
\bar d = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^S d_s, \qquad \tilde d = \text{median}(d_1,\dots,d_S), \qquad W=\frac{1}{S}\sum_s \mathbf 1[d_s>0].
\]

Then report: the mean paired improvement \(\bar d\), median paired improvement \(\tilde d\), 95% BCa or studentized bootstrap CI, win rate \(W\), and a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test. If there are more than two baselines or more than one co-primary metric, either use Friedman-style multiple-comparison logic or control the family-wise error rate with Holm correction. The sign test can remain as a short supplementary sentence. It should not remain the main inferential lens. citeturn8view0turn16view0turn18view1turn18view2

### Fixed score thresholds and strict step-margin claims

The manuscript also uses a “single fixed score threshold chosen before aggregation and applied uniformly across the aggregate,” and treats passing that threshold in every seed as a stricter fixed-static comparison. In formal terms, if \(d^{\text{step}}_s\) is the paired step-loss improvement at seed \(s\), the current logic is functionally
\[
G^{\text{strict}}_s=\mathbf 1[d^{\text{step}}_s>\varepsilon]
\]
for a fixed margin \(\varepsilon\). That is a recognizable superiority-margin structure, but in the current writing it is embedded inside the “gate” vocabulary and is not justified in practical units. fileciteturn0file0

A fixed practical margin can be perfectly valid, but only if it is motivated. In journal form, the paper should state why \(\varepsilon\) matters operationally: for example, because it corresponds to a minimum forecaster improvement that would justify the complexity of dynamic scheduling. If no physical or deployment meaning exists, the threshold should be removed and the strict comparison replaced by a standard paired estimate with CI. If a margin is meaningful, then present it as a **predefined superiority margin** and test it with a one-sided confidence interval rather than “gate passed / gate failed” language. citeturn8view1turn18view2

## Protocol correction and revised analysis workflow

The current manuscript already separates forecaster fitting, policy training, validation, and test chronologically, which is a strong foundation. The revision should preserve that structure while removing three protocol problems that still remain visible from the text: **selection bias**, **baseline leakage into the headline metric**, and **oracle dependence**. The manuscript itself acknowledges the frozen-forecaster limitation, which is helpful; the revision should simply make that limitation methodologically explicit and add one sensitivity analysis that de-risks review. fileciteturn0file0

### Protocol inconsistencies that should be corrected

The first inconsistency is **baseline leakage into the main score**. The primary regime-balanced headline metric uses normalizers derived from feasible fixed-static candidates on the static-selection partition. That means the baseline family partly defines the scale of the primary score. This is the single most important issue to fix. fileciteturn0file0

The second inconsistency is **selection and evaluation entanglement**. The paper selects validation-based static references and then compares learned performance to these selected references under several related metrics, some of which also inherit validation-derived scaling. Selecting baselines on validation is normal; using the same validation-derived structures to define the headline test metric is much harder to defend. fileciteturn0file0

The third inconsistency is **single-evaluator dependence**. The same frozen forecasting model family supplies the reward during policy learning and the final evaluation at test time. The manuscript openly notes that this is not an end-to-end or jointly retrained design. That limitation is acceptable if stated carefully, but the current manuscript uses stronger language than the protocol can support. Under ESWA review, the claim should be narrowed to performance under a fixed evaluation forecaster family, and a sensitivity analysis should be added to show that the result is not entirely a single-model artifact. fileciteturn0file0

The fourth inconsistency is that **privileged-information replay** and **behavioral diagnostics** are mixed into the main claim chain as “gates.” These analyses are valuable, but they should not be required proof obligations for ordinary empirical superiority. Their role should be diagnostic and interpretive. fileciteturn0file0

The fifth inconsistency is that **seed-level inferential units are not cleanly separated from within-seed windows**. Because the time windows within a generated sequence are serially dependent, inference should be performed at the seed level or at a higher independent unit, not by pretending that every test window is an independent replicate. The current manuscript’s seed framing is therefore useful, but the analysis should explicitly say that the paired seed is the inferential unit. This is consistent with current RL evaluation best practice. citeturn17view0turn18view2

### Recommended confirmatory protocol

A corrected confirmatory protocol can remain close to the current design while becoming much clearer.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[Lock hypotheses, metrics, baselines, and margins] --> B[Chronological split per seed]
    B --> C[Fit evaluation forecaster on forecaster-fit split]
    C --> D[Train PD-PPO on policy-train split only]
    D --> E[Select checkpoint and fixed-mask baselines on validation split]
    E --> F[Freeze all model and baseline choices]
    F --> G[Evaluate learned policy and baselines on held-out test split]
    G --> H[Compute seed-level paired differences]
    H --> I[Report CIs, effect sizes, paired tests, and diagnostic analyses]
```

This workflow should be described as the **primary confirmatory protocol**. The purpose is to separate tuning from evaluation and to make the inferential unit explicit. citeturn4view0turn4view4turn18view2

### Required data splits

A journal-appropriate version can use four chronological partitions per seed:

| Split | Recommended size | Purpose | Permitted uses |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Forecaster-fit** | 30–40% | Fit the evaluation forecaster, target scales, and any exogenous regime scales | Forecaster training only |
| **Policy-train** | 40–50% | PPO training, training-time reward computation, imitation-label generation | Policy updates only |
| **Validation** | 10–15% | Checkpoint selection, hyperparameter selection, baseline selection | Model/baseline selection only |
| **Test** | 10–15% | Final reporting | Final metrics only |

If you want to keep the current 35/50/7.5/7.5 split, that is acceptable, but then any standardized regime scales must come from the **forecaster-fit** split or other training-only quantities rather than from selected baseline medians on validation. The important principle is separation of roles, not the exact percentages. fileciteturn0file0 citeturn4view3turn4view4

### Step-by-step procedure for the revised protocol

1. **Predefine the experimental objects.** Before running the final seed aggregate, lock the primary metric, secondary metric, baseline families, regime definitions, regime weights, practical margin if any, and statistical tests. State these choices explicitly in a short “Statistical analysis” subsection. citeturn8view1turn18view2

2. **Fit the evaluation forecaster on the forecaster-fit split only.** Compute target standardization constants and any regime scale constants on this split as well. Do not use fixed-mask validation medians as the main scaling constants. fileciteturn0file0

3. **Train PD-PPO on the policy-train split only.** Training reward may continue to use the frozen evaluation forecaster if the claimed scope is clearly limited to this surrogate-evaluator setting. fileciteturn0file0

### Seed and replicate strategy

The manuscript currently reports 24 seeds, but it is not fully clear from the text whether “seed” combines environment generation, forecaster stochasticity, and policy initialization, or whether it primarily indexes the generated truth sequence. For journal clarity, separate these sources of randomness into **scenario seeds** and **learning seeds**, because RL evaluation literature shows that stochastic training and implementation variation can materially affect conclusions. fileciteturn0file0 citeturn17view0turn18view2

The preferred design is:

- **Scenario seeds:** at least 20 independent generated truth sequences.
- **Learning seeds per scenario:** ideally 3–5 independently initialized PD-PPO runs, all selected on validation using the same locked rule.
- **Baseline stochasticity:** if any baseline is stochastic, run it over the same learning-seed count or show that it is deterministic.

If compute is limited, you can still publish with one learned run per scenario seed, but then the paper should state that the inference is conditional on a single optimization trajectory per scenario. A stronger design averages or models over learning seeds. citeturn17view0turn18view2

### Statistical tests and estimators to report

For each primary or secondary comparison, I recommend the following reporting bundle:

\[
d_s = \text{loss}_{s}(\pi_{\text{baseline}})-\text{loss}_{s}(\pi_{\text{PD-PPO}})
\]

- point estimates: \(\bar d\), median \(d\), and win rate,
- interval estimates: 95% BCa or studentized bootstrap CI across seeds,
- test: paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test,
- supplement: one-sided sign test if you want a direction-only statement,
- effect size: percent improvement and a standardized paired effect size such as \(d_z=\bar d/s_d\) or a rank-based effect size,
- multiplicity control: Holm correction if several baselines and several key metrics are compared simultaneously. citeturn8view0turn8view1turn16view0turn18view1turn18view2

If you adopt multiple learning seeds per scenario, a **cluster bootstrap by scenario seed** is preferable: resample scenario seeds first, then resample learning runs within each selected scenario. That avoids pretending that multiple runs on the same generated truth sequence are independent environments. This recommendation follows directly from the broader RL empirical-design literature on handling multiple sources of variance and avoiding weak statistical evidence. citeturn18view2turn18view3

### Oracle-dependence sensitivity analyses

Because the paper’s reward and final evaluation both depend on a frozen forecaster, the revision should add one explicitly labeled **Sensitivity to evaluation forecaster** subsection. The cleanest options are:

- evaluate the same frozen policies with at least two forecaster families trained on the same forecaster-fit split,
- or train an ensemble of evaluation forecasters and report averaged test metrics with between-forecaster dispersion,
- or add a policy-specific retraining study on a smaller subset of seeds and state clearly that it is a sensitivity analysis, not the main protocol. fileciteturn0file0

This does not require abandoning the current design. It simply narrows the main claim to “PD-PPO improves the downstream forecasting objective under the fixed evaluation forecaster protocol” and then checks whether the direction of the result persists under evaluator variation. That is an analytically stronger and more defensible claim. fileciteturn0file0

## Reviewer checklist and recommended terminology taxonomy

The checklist below is designed around reviewer concerns that are especially likely in ESWA for a paper with RL, simulation, and downstream forecasting components. It also reflects the journal’s documented emphasis on clarity, standard terminology, data transparency, and formal rather than decorative naming. citeturn3view0turn4view0turn4view4

### Reviewer concern checklist

| Likely reviewer concern | What the revised manuscript should show |
|---|---|
| Are the experimental terms standard and formally defined? | A glossary with retained terms only, one mathematical definition per metric, and replacement of “gate” language with standard comparative language |
| Is there one primary outcome? | A clearly labeled primary test metric, secondary metrics, and supplementary diagnostics |
| Does the primary metric leak baseline selection into evaluation? | No baseline-family-derived denominator in the primary endpoint; if scaled, scales are estimated on training-only data |
| Are baselines fairly chosen? | Fixed-mask baselines selected on validation only, then frozen and replayed unchanged on test |
| Are uncertainty and effect size reported? | Paired seed-level differences, 95% CIs, effect sizes, win rates, and paired nonparametric tests |
| Are multiple comparisons handled? | Either one primary comparison or explicit Holm/Friedman-style multiple-comparison handling |
| Is the inferential unit appropriate? | The seed or scenario is clearly identified as the independent evaluation unit |
| Is privileged information separated from deployable baselines? | Event-aware replay renamed as a diagnostic upper bound, not a central baseline |
| Is evaluator dependence acknowledged? | A fixed-evaluator main protocol plus at least one evaluator-sensitivity analysis |
| Are reproducibility and transparency sufficient? | Clear split definitions, code/data availability statement, AI-use disclosure, and tables with non-duplicative information |

### Recommended terminology taxonomy

This taxonomy is the concise “one-page” naming structure I would recommend for the revision. It retains PD-PPO while simplifying the rest of the manuscript into four tiers.

| Tier | Preferred terminology | Avoid | Recommended definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem** | **power-constrained sensor scheduling**, **constrained subset selection**, **regime-balanced forecasting setting** | “dynamic headroom problem,” “specialist shortcut failure mode” in formal claims | Sequential subset selection under hard feasibility constraints with a forecasting-based objective |
| **Model** | **PD-PPO**, **PPO policy with feasibility projection**, **fixed-mask baseline**, **hand-crafted dynamic baseline** | “projection layer” as a novelty claim, “true fixed-static gate” | PD-PPO is the retained method name; execution uses a deterministic map from scores to legal actions |
| **Reward** | **forecast-loss training reward**, **frozen evaluation forecaster**, **switching penalty**, **constraint penalty** | “forecasting oracle” unless clearly scoped, “oracle-ranked” in claims language | Reward is negative forecast loss plus penalties under a forecaster trained before policy learning |
| **Evaluation** | **primary regime-balanced test loss**, **secondary overall test loss**, **event-aware diagnostic upper bound**, **behavioral diagnostics**, **ablation study**, **sensitivity analysis** | **gate**, **passes**, **24/24 proof**, **replay-normalised** | Evaluation should use standard metric names, paired comparisons, interval estimates, and explicitly labeled diagnostic analyses |
