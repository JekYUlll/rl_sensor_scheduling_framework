### Title

**Prediction-Driven Reinforcement Learning for Power-Constrained Sensing-System Scheduling**

### Abstract

Power-constrained multi-sensor systems cannot always operate all measurement channels simultaneously. This paper studies sensing-system scheduling as a prediction-driven constrained decision problem in which a mandatory weather backbone remains active while one specialist channel is selected under power, startup, and minimum-duration operating rules. PD-PPO implements this formulation as masked proximal policy optimization over executable channel masks. The policy uses recent sample-and-hold values and observation masks, channel ready-sampling age, runtime state, previous selections, budget features, and warning scores, while a fixed forecaster provides the multi-step forecast-loss training signal. Evaluation uses a controlled six-channel Antarctic blowing-snow simulation in which particle, flux, and thermal regimes favor different specialist channels. Across 24 evaluation seeds, including a 22-seed post-pilot replication, PD-PPO reduces mean forecast loss by 10.1% and the validation-normalized equal-regime macro score by 7.9% relative to validation-selected fixed schedules, with favorable paired results in every seed. Continuous replay over all 5,242 evaluable epochs in the final partition preserves 24/24 fixed-schedule wins. PD-PPO also outperforms AoI-priority, round-robin, random, and one-step forecast-greedy schedules in every seed, and matched Double-DQN on the macro score in every seed. The warning-score rule and the exact-label reference remain competitive, indicating that event information captures much of the attainable scheduling benefit. Taken together, the results support masked reinforcement learning for forecast-aligned scheduling under hard operating constraints.

### Highlights

- Masked PPO schedules executable channel subsets under hard power rules.  
- A fixed forecaster scores schedules by downstream multi-step forecast loss.  
- PD-PPO beats validation-selected fixed schedules in all 24 evaluation seeds.  
- Continuous full-partition replay preserves the fixed-schedule win in 24/24 seeds.  
- Warning-score and exact-label references bound, but do not overturn, the claim.  

### Introduction first paragraph

Power-constrained sensing systems cannot always keep all measurement channels active simultaneously. In such settings, the scheduler must decide which channels to activate over time while respecting startup requirements and minimum activation durations. Classical scheduling objectives such as age of information and state-estimation uncertainty remain useful when the immediate state estimate is the target. In the present setting, however, schedules are evaluated by the downstream forecasts they support rather than by measurement freshness alone.

### Introduction second paragraph

This change in objective alters the value of a measurement channel. A particle channel can be most informative near transport events, a flux channel can be most informative during mass-transport regimes, and a surface-temperature channel can be most informative during thermal transitions. Because specialist value depends on operating regime, startup delay, observation history, and minimum-duration constraints, the scheduling problem is inherently sequential. The best feasible choice at one epoch need not minimize forecast loss over the decision horizon.

### Contribution paragraph

This paper makes three contributions. First, it formulates power-constrained sensing-system scheduling as a prediction-driven constrained sequential decision problem and shows why forecast loss can induce a different preference ordering from AoI- or uncertainty-based objectives. Second, it develops PD-PPO, which combines masked proximal policy optimization with an executable channel-mask interface and a fixed-forecaster forecast-loss objective. Third, it establishes an evaluation protocol that distinguishes adaptive scheduling value from strong fixed schedules, conventional rule-based schedules, myopic forecast-greedy selection, and privileged event-label references through matched comparisons, full-partition replay, and action-trace diagnostics.

### Methods summary

PD-PPO represents each executable channel subset as a candidate mask and uses online feasibility checks to exclude actions that violate power, startup, or minimum-duration constraints. At each scheduling epoch, the policy receives recent sample-and-hold values and observation masks, channel ready-sampling age, runtime state, previous selections, budget and time features, and warning scores. The policy is trained against multi-step forecast loss from a fixed forecaster fitted on an earlier chronological partition, so all schedule comparisons are made under a common evaluator. During policy training, training-only guide signals and auxiliary context labels are available on the policy-training partition to support representation learning, but final actions are always generated by the masked PPO actor and do not use exact simulator event labels during execution.

### Discussion opening

The results show that, in the one-specialist system studied here, prediction-driven constrained reinforcement learning allocates the scarce measurement slot more effectively than a validation-selected fixed schedule. The advantage is consistent across mean forecast loss and the macro score, across all 24 evaluation seeds, and across the 22-seed post-pilot replication. It also survives continuous full-partition replay and rescoring with a secondary ridge forecaster, which reduces the likelihood that the reported direction is an artifact of window selection or a single evaluator family.

The evidence also clarifies what the method does and does not establish. In this fixed-backbone, one-specialist setting, PD-PPO learns state-dependent specialist allocation that is neither fixed nor cyclic and that outperforms conventional AoI-priority, round-robin, random, and one-step forecast-greedy comparators. At the same time, the warning-score rule and the exact-label reference remain competitive, showing that event information accounts for much of the attainable scheduling benefit. The paper therefore supports forecast-aligned constrained RL under the reported operating rules, rather than claiming universal superiority over all context-aware heuristics.

### Conclusion

This paper formulates power-constrained sensing-system scheduling as downstream forecast optimization over executable channel subsets. PD-PPO implements this formulation as masked proximal policy optimization using partial observations, channel ready-sampling age, runtime state, previous selections, budget features, and warning scores, while enforcing power, startup, and minimum-duration rules directly at the action interface. In the reported six-channel simulation, PD-PPO reduces mean forecast loss by 10.1% and the macro score by 7.9% relative to validation-selected fixed schedules, with favorable paired results in all 24 evaluation seeds. The same fixed-schedule direction remains under continuous full-partition replay over all 5,242 evaluable epochs in the final partition.

Matched comparisons further bound the interpretation of this result. PD-PPO is more consistent than matched Double-DQN on the same state, reward, and feasible action interface, and the fixed-schedule comparison remains favorable under a secondary ridge forecaster. The reward-control comparisons should be interpreted within the shared training scaffold, since they retain the same training-only guide signals and auxiliary labels. The warning-score rule and the exact-label reference show that event information captures much of the attainable scheduling benefit in this simulation. Taken together, the results indicate that masked sequential learning can translate forecast requirements into feasible sensor-activation decisions when different operating regimes favor incompatible measurements under a hard power budget.
