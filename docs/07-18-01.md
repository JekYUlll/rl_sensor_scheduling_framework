# Detailed Revision Audit for the Current ESWA Manuscript

## Current manuscript position

The current draft is already structured as a **methods-and-protocol paper**, not a benchmark catalog. Its abstract and introduction clearly frame the work as a **prediction-driven constrained scheduling formulation**, implemented with feasibility-masked PPO and evaluated through a replay protocol that separates fixed, myopic, and privileged references. That is the right backbone to preserve. fileciteturn0file2L27-L33 fileciteturn0file2L128-L138

The strongest parts of the paper should remain central. The formulation is not merely “PPO for scheduling”; it explicitly distinguishes forecast loss from AoI and covariance objectives, and Proposition 2 gives the paper a real conceptual identity beyond empirical tuning. That theoretical distinction is one of the manuscript’s most defensible contributions and should not be shortened away during revision. fileciteturn0file2L436-L492

The main held-out result is also already organized in the right hierarchy. The current results section separates the validation-selected fixed schedule as the main confirmatory comparator, treats one-step forecast-greedy as a sequential-value boundary, and isolates the context-alert bandit as a strong context-aware stress test rather than trying to hide it. That is scientifically honest and should stay. fileciteturn0file2L881-L913

At the same time, the draft is **not yet submission-safe** in its current form. The two biggest remaining risks are not “writing style” but **reproducibility** and **experimental disentanglement**. The paper currently says the policy observes estimator state, uncertainty, freshness, previous selection, budget state, and event cues, but it still does not fully specify how those online quantities are produced. It also defines the baseline families mostly by role rather than by executable algorithm. Those are the first things a serious reviewer will attack. fileciteturn0file2L505-L528 fileciteturn0file2L572-L593 fileciteturn0file2L715-L760

A second important boundary is branch consistency. The uploaded `CHANGELOG.md` shows that there is also a **separate no-warmup / deployment-constrained paper line**, with its own isolated paper track and later manuscript integration. That material should **not** be back-ported wholesale into the current Antarctic fixed-backbone manuscript unless the whole paper is intentionally re-based onto that operational branch. Otherwise the claim logic will fracture. fileciteturn0file1L1480-L1497 fileciteturn0file1L2242-L2249

## High-priority scientific revisions

The first revision block is the **estimator-and-observation chain**. In the current framework section, the manuscript states that the scheduler receives an estimator summary and that the fixed forecaster consumes estimated state, observation mask, freshness features, derived physical features, and time context, but it does not specify the estimator update map, how powered-off channels are handled, what observation noise model is assumed, or how uncertainty is propagated over time. The reader can infer that these quantities exist, but not reconstruct them. fileciteturn0file2L505-L528 fileciteturn0file2L572-L593

HERMES should therefore add a compact but explicit subsection, placed immediately after current Section 4.1, titled something like **Estimator State, Freshness, and Uncertainty Update**. That subsection should define, in equations or exact algorithm form, the following four objects: the per-epoch estimate state; the rule for active-channel observation assimilation; the update rule for inactive channels, including freshness/AoI increments; and the uncertainty variable used by the policy and forecaster. If the implementation uses a heuristic estimator rather than a Kalman-style filter, that is acceptable, but the exact rule must still be stated. This is not benchmark inflation. It is the missing half of the method definition.

The second block is the **baseline execution definition**. The present comparator table describes roles rather than algorithms: fixed schedule, rule-based schedules, forecast-greedy, context-alert stress test, and event-label reference are all named, but their decision order, tie-breaking logic, thresholding, minimum-on handling, and feasible-mask projection are not written in executable form in the main paper. fileciteturn0file2L715-L760 HERMES should keep the current taxonomy table, but add a new appendix table called something like **Executable Baseline Definitions**, with one row per baseline and these columns: online inputs, score function, evaluation horizon, tie-break rule, projection into feasible masks, start-up handling, minimum-duration handling, stochasticity source, and validation-selected parameters if any. Then add two or three lines of pseudocode per nontrivial baseline. This one change will close a large reproducibility gap without turning the paper into a benchmark survey.

The third block is the **claim scope around rule-based baselines**. Right now the abstract still says PD-PPO improves over “rule-based references,” while the results simultaneously report the context-alert bandit as competitive and negative on average in the final 24-seed summary. fileciteturn0file2L19-L21 fileciteturn0file2L29-L33 fileciteturn0file2L903-L904 That wording is too broad. The correct repair is not to weaken the paper into vagueness; it is to **separate conventional rule baselines from context-aware hand-coded stress tests**. HERMES should revise all summary-layer wording to say that PD-PPO beats **validation-selected fixed schedules and conventional AoI / stale-channel / random rule-based comparators**, while the handcrafted context-alert policy is reported separately as a strong context-aware stress test. That preserves a strong claim while removing an avoidable overstatement.

The fourth block is the **seed wording**. The setup section is already honest: it states that the reported aggregate uses seeds 117–140, and that seeds 117 and 122 were involved in the preliminary configuration pivot, with the remaining 22 seeds serving as the unchanged post-pilot replication. fileciteturn0file2L825-L833 But the abstract and introduction still use phrasing that reads like fully independent 24-seed holdout evidence. fileciteturn0file2L19-L21 fileciteturn0file2L118-L127 HERMES should standardize this everywhere to: **“24 evaluation seeds, including a 22-seed post-pilot replication.”** That wording is both precise and strong enough for ESWA.

The fifth block is the **simulator specification**. The truth-sequence generation section currently says that events are produced by a semi-Markov process coupled to wind exceedance and that generator checks cover scalar distributions, event frequencies, durations, flux–wind scaling, particle correlation, and spectra. fileciteturn0file2L794-L815 That is good as a domain summary, but not enough for independent reconstruction. HERMES should add a simulator appendix that contains: the semi-Markov state set and transition logic; event duration distributions; latent-variable coupling equations or update equations; per-channel observation noise parameters; and the exact procedure for generating station-side event-context proxies. A single full parameter table is worth far more than extra prose here.

## Claim architecture that should be preserved

The paper should **not** be rewritten as a benchmark paper, and it should **not** be softened into a generic “PPO works sometimes” narrative. The present contribution structure is close to correct and should be strengthened, not flattened. The right hierarchy is already visible in the manuscript: formulation, masked implementation, and replay protocol. fileciteturn0file2L128-L138

What HERMES should preserve at the center is this:

The primary contribution is a **prediction-driven constrained scheduling formulation**. The manuscript already justifies why forecast loss is not interchangeable with AoI or covariance and why dynamic value arises when specialist-optimal regimes are incompatible. Those are not peripheral theoretical ornaments; they are the conceptual core of the paper. fileciteturn0file2L318-L386 fileciteturn0file2L436-L492

The secondary contribution is the **feasible-mask RL instantiation**. Here the paper must stay accurate: the novelty is not “inventing PPO.” The novelty is instantiating the forecast-aligned constrained scheduling problem with executable mask selection under hard online feasibility checks. HERMES should therefore slightly reduce “PPO innovation” wording but keep the method claim strong by phrasing it around the **decision geometry**: candidate executable masks, online feasibility filtering, delayed forecast reward, and constrained deployment semantics. That is stronger and more defensible than algorithm-brand emphasis.

The tertiary contribution is the **replay protocol that separates adaptive value from fixed, myopic, and privileged references**. This stays important, but it must remain third, not first. It supports the method claim; it should not eclipse it.

The context-alert bandit should remain in the paper, but as a **stress-test reference**. The manuscript already positions it correctly in the results section as competitive rather than confirmatory. fileciteturn0file2L881-L904 HERMES should keep that treatment and remove any lingering wording that suggests it belongs in the same family as AoI/stale/random rules.

## Experimental closure with minimal inflation

The single largest experimental completeness risk is the absence of a control that separates **reward alignment** from **PPO machinery**, and the absence of a second learning baseline that shares the same masked action interface. The current ablation table only varies auxiliary terms inside PD-PPO; it does not test whether forecast reward itself is the decisive factor under the same policy architecture. fileciteturn0file2L1074-L1090

The most efficient fix is not to run a large benchmark zoo. It is to add two **surgical controls**.

The first is a **same-architecture reward control**: keep the exact same masked PPO architecture, feasible action set, observation interface, and training protocol, but train alternative versions with an AoI-based reward and an uncertainty-based reward. The paper has already argued theoretically that these objectives are not equivalent to forecast loss. fileciteturn0file2L436-L492 A same-architecture reward comparison will convert that theoretical point into direct algorithmic evidence. This is the most important missing experiment and should be treated as mandatory if the submission aims to be read as an algorithm/framework paper rather than a protocol study.

The second is one **additional masked learning baseline**, preferably the lightest one consistent with the current action interface. If engineering cost is a concern, masked A2C is the cleanest candidate. If code exists or can be added safely, masked DQN is also acceptable, but the paper does not need both to make the point. What matters is that the comparator uses the same candidate-mask action surface and the same hard feasibility interface. Without that, a reviewer can still ask whether the win is mostly “PPO luck.” The current comparator set — fixed, rule-based, forecast-greedy, and context-alert — is good for behavioral interpretation, but it does not fully isolate the learning method. fileciteturn0file2L715-L760 fileciteturn0file2L881-L904

The generalization block should also be expanded, but compactly. Right now the empirical scope is one specialist slot, one fixed forecaster family, one main power budget, and a local higher-flux sensitivity check. fileciteturn0file2L531-L546 fileciteturn0file2L1098-L1101 That is enough for a mechanism paper, but not yet strong enough for a robustness-minded ESWA review. HERMES should prioritize one of two additions: either a **budget sensitivity** table with one lower and one higher budget under the same protocol, or a **second frozen forecaster rescoring** experiment using a different forecasting family on the same held-out trajectories. The latter is especially valuable because the paper explicitly relies on a frozen forecaster during both training and evaluation. fileciteturn0file2L523-L528 If only one can be done pre-submission, the same-architecture reward ablation is still more important than either robustness add-on.

## HERMES editing brief

HERMES should revise the paper in four passes.

In the first pass, it should make the **summary-layer wording exact**. Everywhere that currently says or implies “24 independent held-out seeds” should be changed to “24 evaluation seeds, including a 22-seed post-pilot replication,” in the abstract, introduction, figure captions, and conclusion. Wherever the paper currently says “rule-based references” at the summary level, it should be narrowed to “conventional rule-based references” or named as AoI / stale-channel / random schedules, while reporting the context-alert bandit separately as a strong context-aware stress test. This is a precision upgrade, not a claim retreat. fileciteturn0file2L19-L21 fileciteturn0file2L118-L127 fileciteturn0file2L825-L833 fileciteturn0file2L903-L904

In the second pass, it should close the **method reproducibility chain**. It should add one subsection defining the estimator and uncertainty update, and one appendix table defining all baselines as executable procedures. The main text should still stay compact; the details belong in appendices, with one short sentence in the method section pointing there. This preserves a tight core manuscript while making replication possible.

In the third pass, it should **rebalance Discussion**. The current discussion is competent but short relative to the heavy problem formulation and result sections. It should gain five short paragraphs and lose repeated result restatements. The five additions should cover: when static designs remain strong; why the context-alert heuristic is strong in this benchmark; what “sequential value beyond one-step greedy” means operationally; what the online computation burden is at deployment; and how the single-specialist setting scales to multiple specialist slots. The current discussion already starts well on claim scope and auxiliary signals, but it needs more operational interpretation and less “restating the result.” fileciteturn0file2L1102-L1107

In the fourth pass, it should repair the **appendix and production layer**. The codex pagination note about Appendix H still needs to be applied. More broadly, HERMES should ensure that appendix tables begin on the same page as their section headers where practical, that data-availability language does not promise unfinished repositories, and that no separate paper-track material from the changelog is silently mixed into the manuscript. The changelog makes clear that the no-warmup / env-dwell / h75 line is a different evidence branch with its own manuscript and should not be merged casually into the present paper. fileciteturn0file1L1480-L1497 fileciteturn0file1L2242-L2249

## Production priorities and non-goals

Before submission, the manuscript should absolutely complete the following: estimator specification, baseline specification, corrected seed wording, corrected rule-based claim wording, and at least one same-architecture reward-control experiment. Those are the decisive items for reducing rejection risk while preserving the paper’s current strength.

The manuscript should preferably also add one masked learning baseline and one compact robustness addition, but those come after the reproducibility chain and reward disentanglement.

Three things should **not** happen during revision.

The paper should not delete the context-alert bandit. It is currently important evidence of claim boundaries and strengthens credibility precisely because it is strong. fileciteturn0file2L903-L904

The paper should not be broadened into an omnibus benchmark article. The present formulation–implementation–protocol triad is already a good ESWA shape. fileciteturn0file2L128-L138

The paper should not import later no-warmup / deployable-dwell results from another branch just to manufacture stronger tables. The changelog explicitly records those as a separate paper line and separate active manuscript. fileciteturn0file1L1480-L1497 fileciteturn0file1L2242-L2249

The right end state is a **stronger method paper with sharper reproducibility and cleaner experimental isolation**, not a weaker claim and not a broader benchmark.
