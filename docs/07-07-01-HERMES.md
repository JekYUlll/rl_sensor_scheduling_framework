## Core editorial observations

The manuscript contains the ingredients for a strong method paper: a forecast-loss objective for scheduling, a feasibility-masked action space with hard operating rules, a fixed forecaster serving as both training signal and common evaluator, and a replay-based protocol that separates adaptive scheduling from fixed-schedule shortcuts. However, the current draft foregrounds the benchmark and protocol mechanics more than the method contribution.

The revision should strengthen the paper around its method-level claim — prediction-driven constrained reinforcement learning for sensor scheduling — while de-emphasizing benchmark narration, collapsing duplicated protocol and figure material, and moving non-confirmatory development material to an appendix.

## Problem areas in the current draft

### Emphasis and structure

- The abstract and introduction foreground the controlled Antarctic benchmark and repeatedly restate setting-specific boundaries, rather than leading with the method.
- The paper has two chronology/protocol figures that overlap in purpose.
- The specialist-use heatmap appears in two places.
- Discussion 7.2 leaves room for future threshold/context policies without integrating known stress-test boundaries into the current contribution logic.
- Development-only material (context-aware extension variants) remains in the main text rather than an appendix.

### Terminology and framing

- The manuscript now uses consistent terms after the 2026-07-02 terminology pass: "fixed schedule", "weather backbone", "selectable specialist sensor", "action-trace checks", "macro score normalized by fixed-schedule references."
- Some internal framing around the context-alert bandit as a "boundary baseline" rather than a "failure" needs careful wording. The manuscript should not claim universal superiority over all context-aware heuristics, but should state clearly what PD-PPO does improve over: fixed schedules, simple dynamic references, and myopic forecast-greedy selection.

## Suggested editorial directions

These are directions for human review, not verbatim replacement text.

### Abstract direction

The abstract should lead with the method claim rather than the benchmark. A possible structure: (1) what problem PD-PPO addresses — power-limited sensor scheduling with forecast-value objective and executable-mask constraints, (2) what the method does — feasibility-masked PPO policy with a fixed forecaster as reward and evaluator, (3) what the protocol isolates — the value of observation patterns independent of forecaster retraining, (4) what the held-out result shows — improvement over fixed schedules and simple dynamic references on the controlled benchmark, and (5) the boundary — a handcrafted context rule remains competitive.

### Introduction direction

The introduction should move the method claim to the front. The benchmark description — mandatory backbone, one specialist slot, event-sensitive channels — should appear as the testbed, not as the lead story. The current strongest sentences — about forecast loss ranking schedules differently from AoI or covariance, about executable mask geometry as part of the deployed policy, about the frozen forecaster as a common evaluator — should move earlier.

### Methods direction

The Methods section could benefit from a compact two-paragraph framing at the start, before implementation detail. The first paragraph should state the forecast-value control objective and how the reward is produced. The second paragraph should state the feasibility-masked action space and the replay-based held-out protocol. The current material about chronology splits and protocol detail can then follow as implementation exposition.

### Results reorganization suggestions

The current Results section would benefit from a clearer separation between confirmatory comparisons and diagnostics. A possible structure:

1. **Main held-out comparisons** — the primary confirmatory table first, with one paragraph stating the improvement over validation-selected fixed schedules and simple dynamic references.
2. **Sequential value beyond myopic forecast-greedy** — the comparison against one-step forecast-greedy as the key method-level evidence that sequential credit assignment matters.
3. **Regime decomposition and stress test** — the per-regime table as the diagnostic bridge from the aggregate result, followed by a paragraph on the context-aware handcrafted baseline as a boundary test.
4. **Behavioral diagnostics** — one figure and one paragraph showing that the learned policy is not a degenerate constant mask or simple cycle.
5. **Ablation and robustness** — one short paragraph in the main text; move changed-mixture robustness to an appendix if space is tight.

### Figure consolidation

- Consolidate the two chronology/protocol figures into one.
- Keep only one specialist-use heatmap.
- The current behavioral diagnostics figure is strong and should remain.
- A figure comparing PD-PPO against forecast-greedy selection (if not already present) would strengthen the "sequential value" claim.

### Context-aware extension appendix

The context-aware extension variants (CA-PD-PPO and its development iterations) should move to an appendix. The appendix prose should be short: one paragraph explaining that a bounded development study explored clean architectural changes without altering the core method identity, none of the variants passed the prespecified threshold for a confirmatory final evaluation, and the results are reported as non-confirmatory development evidence.

### Claim boundaries

The manuscript correctly limits its claims. These boundaries should be preserved:
- The result is tied to a fixed evaluation forecaster.
- The result is tied to generated truth sequences.
- The result is tied to a narrow benchmark geometry (one mandatory backbone, one specialist slot).
- The context-aware handcrafted baseline remains a competitive boundary, not a failed comparison.
- The paper does not claim universal superiority over all context-aware heuristics.
