# Editorial Review of the Current ESWA Manuscript

## Executive summary

The manuscript is now scientifically coherent and much closer to submission quality than the earlier drafts, but it still carries a noticeable layer of lab-internal wording. The remaining editorial risk is not the science; it is the *presentation*: too many invented hyphenated compounds, too many near-synonymous names for the same comparator or metric, too much “meta” prose about what a control *assesses* rather than what it *shows*, and a main figure that still explains too much inside the artwork instead of in the caption. Those issues are visible in the title and abstract, in Figure 1 on page 4, in the metric/comparator language across Sections 5–7, and in several appendix-facing terms that leak back into the main paper. fileciteturn0file0

The most important editorial rule set for this revision round is straightforward. First, keep only field-standard hyphenation and remove lab-internal compounds unless they are truly necessary. Second, choose one canonical name for each metric, baseline, and diagnostic, and use it everywhere. Third, replace abstract nouns such as “sensitivity,” “value,” and “result direction” with direct claims about *what improved*, *what changed*, or *what was tested*. Fourth, move explanation out of figures and into captions; Elsevier’s guidance is to keep text inside figures to a minimum, use clear captions, and submit vector artwork with embedded fonts, preferably PDF or EPS for line art. Fifth, verify that no figure or graphical element was created or altered with generative AI, because ESWA follows Elsevier’s policy prohibiting generative AI for submitted figures and artwork unless the AI image itself is the object of study. citeturn0search5turn0search0turn0search1turn0search2turn0search3turn2search0

What follows is a rigorous editorial pass on the *latest manuscript text available in this thread*, with concrete replacements rather than abstract style advice. The review is organized around four operational goals: remove coined compounds, simplify AI-styled phrasing, standardize terminology, and de-clutter the figures and captions. The recommendations below are intentionally strict because ESWA reviewers will read the paper as a methods paper, not as an internal project memo. fileciteturn0file0

### Redesigned main-figure layout

The best redesign for the main figure is a **three-tier protocol-layout figure** that removes nearly all sentence fragments from the drawing itself. The figure should have:

- a **top row** with four equal-width boxes: *Forecaster training* → *Policy training* → *Validation* → *Final evaluation*;
- a **middle row** with the runtime loop: *Observation update* → *Build scheduler state* → *Policy logits* → *Feasibility mask* → *Execute channel subset*;
- a **bottom dashed row** labeled *Training only*: *Fixed forecaster* → *Forecast loss* → *PPO update*;
- one side note below the middle row: **Weather backbone always on; one specialist chosen at each step**;
- one tiny legend: **solid arrows = online execution; dashed arrows = training only**.

That design would preserve the scientific message of the current Figure 1 but cut the in-figure text by roughly half. It also aligns better with the journal’s instruction to keep figure text minimal and let the caption do the explanatory work. fileciteturn0file0 citeturn0search5turn0search0turn0search1turn0search2
