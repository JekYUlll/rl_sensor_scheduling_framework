# Length Reduction and Restructuring Recommendations

The paper currently runs to 76 pages (including appendices). For Cold Regions Science and Technology the target is roughly 40–50 manuscript pages. The analysis below identifies every section where text can be cut, merged, or moved to supplementary material, with specific find/replace or delete instructions. Estimated savings are given per section; the total realistic saving is **25–30 pages** without losing any scientific content.

## Section-by-Section Cuts

### §1 Introduction (save ~1.5 pages)

Merge them into one tight paragraph of 6–8 sentences. The current text repeats "blowing-snow events" and "warm-up" three times each before the problem is even stated.

**Cut 2 — Shorten the three-challenge paragraph.** The paragraph listing challenges (i), (ii), (iii) before the Contributions heading is 12 lines. It can be reduced to 4 lines by removing the parenthetical elaborations; the elaborations are already covered in §5.

**Cut 3 — Tighten the Paper Organisation paragraph.** The current "Paper Organisation" paragraph is 8 lines of boilerplate section-by-section summary. Replace with 2 lines: *"Section 2 reviews related work; Sections 3–5 formulate the problem, environment, and algorithm; Section 6 reports experiments; Section 7 discusses limitations; Section 8 concludes."*

### §2 Related Work (save ~2 pages)

The four subsections (§2.1 Sensor Scheduling, §2.2 Deep RL for Scheduling, §2.3 Polar Meteorology, §2.4 Synthetic Time Series) are each written as mini-surveys. For a methods paper, related work should position the contribution, not survey the field.

**Cut 4 — Merge §2.1 and §2.2 into one subsection** titled "Sensor Scheduling and Deep RL." The key positioning sentences are: (a) classical KF/AoI methods assume linear-Gaussian dynamics; (b) deep RL methods handle non-linear settings but have not addressed warm-up-aware combinatorial scheduling with prediction-driven rewards. Everything else in the two subsections is background that the reader does not need to evaluate the paper's claims. Target: 1 page for the merged subsection (currently 2.5 pages).

**Cut 5 — Reduce §2.3 to 1 paragraph.** The polar meteorology subsection (Wang et al. 2021, Lim et al. 2021, Liu et al. 2024, Ding et al. 2025, Amory 2020, Monrad-Krohn et al. 2026, Lenaerts et al. 2023, Wille et al. 2023) is 1.5 pages. The only sentences that are load-bearing for the paper are: (a) the TCN/TFT motivation for the frozen oracle, and (b) the AntAWS and Amory 2020 references for simulation calibration. Reduce to 5–6 sentences.

**Cut 6 — Absorb §2.4 into §4.** The synthetic time series subsection (Aloni et al. 2024, Wang et al. 2025) describes the DFT-phase-randomisation method that is already explained in detail in §4.2. Move the one positioning sentence ("We use the method of Aloni et al. (2024) because it preserves marginal distributions and PSD without a parametric joint model") to the opening of §4.2 and delete §2.4 entirely.

### §3 Problem Formulation (save ~3 pages)

§3 is the most over-written section. It has 7 subsections for what is essentially a standard CMDP formulation with three non-standard elements (warm-up FSM, prediction-driven reward, action masking).

**Cut 7 — Merge §3.1 and §3.2 into one subsection** titled "Sensors and Hardware Prototype." §3.1 (Sensors, Variables, Warm-Up Dynamics) is 1.5 pages; §3.2 (Hardware Prototype) is 1.5 pages. The hardware prototype text is important for credibility but does not need its own subsection. Merge: keep the sensor FSM (Figure 2 and Eq. 1–3), keep the prototype paragraph (RS485, acquisition scripts, datasheet anchors), and delete the repeated explanation of why FC4 has a high logical cost (it appears in §3.1, §3.2, and Table 1 footnote — keep only the Table 1 footnote version).

**Cut 8 — Shorten §3.3 (Hard Feasibility Constraints).** The three equations (4–6) are essential. The surrounding prose (6 paragraphs) repeats the distinction between fixed-budget and energy-account three times. Keep: one paragraph explaining the three constraints and the note that B and B_peak are fixed scenario parameters. Delete: the paragraph about the empty action always being feasible (obvious), the paragraph about temperature-dependent safety interlocks (already noted as out of scope), and the forward reference to §6.6 (the reader will reach it).

**Cut 9 — Shorten §3.4 (State Representation).** The state vector equation (7) and the explanation of z_t are essential. The final two sentences ("This state is Markov with respect to the simulated environment because...") are obvious given the construction and can be deleted.

**Cut 10 — Shorten §3.5 (Prediction-Driven Reward).** The forecast target set (Eq. 8) and the loss formula (Eq. 10–11) are essential. The paragraph listing the nine target variables in prose form (after Eq. 8) duplicates the equation. Delete the prose list; keep only the equation.

**Cut 11 — Merge §3.6 and §3.7.** §3.6 (CMDP Objective) states the optimisation problem (Eq. 12–14) and explains the baselines. §3.7 (Why Forecast Reward Is Not an AoI or Kalman Proxy) states Proposition 1 and its proof sketch. These are logically sequential and can be one subsection titled "CMDP Objective and Reward Non-Equivalence." The proof sketch of Proposition 1 (currently 12 lines) can be reduced to 6 lines by removing the sentence "The full construction follows the same mechanism observed empirically..." (this is a forward reference that adds no logical content). Remark 1 (the long-term average-power variant) can be moved to a footnote.

### §6 Experiments (save ~4 pages)

§6 has 12 subsections. Several can be merged or shortened.

**Cut 16 — Merge §6.1 (Evaluation Protocol) and §6.2 (Baselines) into one subsection** titled "Evaluation Protocol and Baselines." Both are short (1 page each) and logically belong together. The five-item bulleted list of baselines can be replaced by a prose paragraph of 6 lines.

**Cut 17 — Shorten §6.4 (Budget Sensitivity).** The current text (1 page) describes the budget sweep results that are already visible in Table 3. Reduce to 3–4 sentences: state the range of PD-PPO improvement over round-robin (2.6%–3.2%), note the AoI degradation at higher budgets, and cite Table 3.

**Cut 18 — Shorten §6.7 (Oracle Reward Signal Reliability).** This section (1.5 pages, Table 7) is a development diagnostic on the V2 suite. The key finding (oracle FW-MAE decreases monotonically from k=1 to k=8) can be stated in 3 sentences. The V2 absolute numbers (full obs 0.363, static 0.389, PD-PPO 0.390) are not comparable to the V3.1 main results and should not be in the main paper. **Move Table 7 and its surrounding text to a supplementary appendix** or reduce to a single sentence: "Oracle FW-MAE decreases monotonically with the number of active channels (k=1: 0.696 to k=8: 0.385 in the V2 diagnostic suite), confirming that the reward signal is non-degenerate."

**Cut 19 — Shorten §6.8 (Physical-Unit Interpretation).** Table 8 (per-variable MAE in physical units) is useful but the surrounding prose (1 page) largely restates the table. Reduce to 2 sentences introducing the table and 1 sentence noting the air-temperature and wind-speed improvements relative to AoI.

**Cut 20 — Shorten §6.9 (Training Diagnostics).** The current text (0.5 pages + Figure 9) is a single paragraph. It can be reduced to 2 sentences: "Figure 9 shows stable training: entropy remains positive throughout, the prior-KL regulariser stays bounded, and no divergence is observed across seeds."

**Cut 21 — Merge §6.10 (A2 Staged Diagnostic) and §6.11 (A1 Full Ablation) into one subsection** titled "Component Ablation." The staged diagnostic (Table 9) and the remove-one ablation (Table 10) are complementary and should be presented together. The current text explaining the difference between A1 and A2 (3 paragraphs) can be reduced to 1 paragraph.

**Cut 22 — Shorten §6.12 (Hyperparameter Sensitivity).** The 3×3 grid result (Figure 10) is already compact. The surrounding prose (1 page) can be reduced to 3 sentences: state the grid, state that all cells are within 2.5% of the default, and note that the lowest mean is at (0.1, 0.5).

## Structural Restructuring (beyond cuts)

### Merge §3.2 and §4.1 into a single "Hardware and Simulation Grounding" subsection

Both §3.2 and §4.1 describe the hardware prototype and explain why normalised cost units are used instead of watt measurements. They are currently separated by §3.3–§3.7 and §4.2–§4.4. The cleanest fix is:

- Keep §3.2 as a short paragraph (4–5 lines) noting that a bench prototype validates the RS485 acquisition path and that field deployment is planned for September 2026.
- Delete §4.1 entirely; move its two statistical-source bullet points to the opening of §4.2.

### Consolidate the AoI framing

The paper currently explains the AoI framing in three places: §3.7 (Proposition 1), §6.3 (fixed-budget results), and §6.6 (energy-account results). The key distinction — AoI is a weak baseline in fixed-budget but a strong baseline in energy-account — should be stated once, clearly, in §6.3, with a forward reference to §6.6. The repeated explanations in §3.7 and §7.2 can be shortened.
