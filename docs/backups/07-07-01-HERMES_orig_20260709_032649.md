# HERMES Revision Brief for PD-PPO

## Executive summary

The paper already contains the ingredients for a strong method paper: a forecast-loss objective for scheduling, a feasibility-masked action space with hard operating rules, a fixed forecaster used as both training signal and common evaluator, and a replay-based protocol that separates adaptive scheduling from fixed-mask shortcuts and cyclic behavior. The current draft also already states the right boundary conditions: the result is tied to a fixed evaluation forecaster, generated truth sequences, and a narrow benchmark geometry. Those are strengths when framed as methodological control rather than as apologetic limitation language. fileciteturn0file0

The revision should therefore do three things at once. It should sharpen the paper around a method-level claim—**prediction-driven constrained reinforcement learning for sensor scheduling**—while de-emphasizing benchmark narration, collapsing duplicated protocol/figure material, and moving non-confirmatory development material such as the CA-PD-PPO dev2 wave to Appendix F with very short prose. The manuscript should read as a compact framework paper with a controlled benchmark, not as a benchmark paper with a framework attached. fileciteturn0file0

## Source basis and editorial strategy

The manuscript’s strongest reusable spine is already visible in the abstract, Introduction, Section 3.4, and Section 4: the paper argues that forecast loss can rank schedules differently from AoI/covariance objectives, that executable mask geometry is part of the deployed policy, and that the forecaster is frozen across training and held-out evaluation so schedule comparisons are about observation patterns rather than forecaster retraining. The current draft also already distinguishes fixed-mask replay, event-label replay, rule-based references, and behavioral diagnostics in a way that supports a method contribution rather than a pure task report. fileciteturn0file0

The main editorial problem is emphasis. The present abstract and introduction foreground the “controlled Antarctic benchmark” and repeatedly restate setting-specific boundaries, while Figures 1–2 both spend space on chronology/protocol, Figures 5–6 repeat the same specialist-use heatmap, and Discussion 7.2 leaves room for future handwritten threshold/context policies without integrating the now-known bandit stress-test boundary into the paper’s current contribution logic. The revision should pull the method forward, compress protocol exposition, and relocate development-only evidence to the appendix. fileciteturn0file0

## Exact replacement text for HERMES

### Abstract replacement

Use this as the **entire abstract**. It is 113 words.

> We propose PD-PPO, a prediction-driven constrained reinforcement learning framework for power-limited sensor scheduling. The policy selects only executable sensor masks through online feasibility masking, so power, startup, and minimum-duration rules are enforced at decision time. A fixed multi-step forecaster supplies the reward and the common held-out evaluator, which isolates the value of the induced observation pattern. On a controlled microclimate benchmark with a mandatory backbone and one selectable specialist channel, PD-PPO consistently improves over validation-selected fixed masks, simple dynamic references, and one-step forecast-greedy scheduling across 24 held-out seeds. A strong handcrafted context-alert policy remains competitive, while a context-aware extension narrows that gap without establishing stable dominance.

This replacement keeps the method claim strong, removes the benchmark-first tone, and avoids universal-superiority language. It is consistent with the current manuscript’s forecast-loss framing, feasibility masking, fixed forecaster, and held-out replay protocol. fileciteturn0file0

### Introduction opening replacement

Replace the current opening pages of the Introduction with this compact opening. It keeps the same technical core while reducing benchmark-heavy exposition.

> Power-limited sensing systems often face a structured scheduling problem: a compact backbone should remain online, while a small specialist budget must be allocated among channels whose value depends on what will happen next. In this setting, the relevant question is not only which measurement is freshest or most uncertain now, but which executable sensor choice will reduce downstream forecast error over the next prediction horizon.
>
> This paper studies that problem as prediction-driven constrained reinforcement learning. We evaluate sensor schedules by multi-step forecast loss, restrict decisions to executable channel masks that satisfy power and operating rules, and compare policies under a fixed forecaster and replay-based held-out protocol. The benchmark is intentionally narrow: a mandatory meteorological backbone, one specialist slot, and event-sensitive channels whose forecast value changes across particle, flux, and thermal regimes. That controlled geometry is used to test a method claim about sequential forecast-value scheduling, not to present a new benchmark for its own sake.

This opening better matches the strongest technical statements already developed later in Sections 3–4 and avoids over-investing the introduction in benchmark mechanics. fileciteturn0file0

### Contributions replacement

Replace the current numbered contribution list with the following **three-line bullet list**.

- **Prediction-driven constrained scheduling formulation.** We formulate sensor scheduling as downstream forecast-value optimization under a mandatory backbone, limited specialist capacity, and hard online feasibility rules.
- **Feasibility-masked RL implementation.** We develop PD-PPO, a masked categorical PPO policy that selects only executable sensor masks and learns from forecast loss produced by a fixed forecaster.
- **Replay-based evaluation protocol.** We separate forecaster fitting, policy learning, fixed-reference selection, and final testing, and use fixed-mask replay, myopic and rule-based references, behavioral audits, and a context-aware stress test to identify genuine adaptive scheduling gains.

### Methods framing replacement

Insert the following **two-paragraph framing** at the start of the Methods section, before implementation detail. It can replace the current long lead-in around Figures 1–2 and Section 4.1/4.2 transitions.

> PD-PPO is designed around a forecast-value control objective rather than an information-freshness proxy. At each scheduling epoch, the policy observes an estimator summary that includes state estimates, uncertainty, channel freshness, previous mask state, budget state, and online context cues. The reward is produced by a fixed multi-step forecaster that scores the partial observation trajectory induced by the executed schedule. This makes the learning target explicit: a sensor activation is valuable when it improves downstream prediction quality, even if it is not the freshest or lowest-uncertainty choice under AoI- or covariance-style criteria.
>
> The action space is a finite set of candidate sensor masks, but only masks that are executable at the current epoch may be selected. Online feasibility masking removes actions that violate the power budget, startup cost, or minimum-duration rules before sampling or greedy execution. The same fixed forecaster and the same replay interface are then used in held-out evaluation, with chronological separation between forecaster fitting, policy learning, fixed-reference selection, and final testing. This protocol isolates the effect of the induced observation pattern and prevents final-window tuning or hidden fallback dynamics from being confused with adaptive scheduling.

This text tightens the method story around the exact concepts already present in Section 4, Algorithm 1, Table 4, and the chronological split figures. fileciteturn0file0

## Discussion, appendix, and cover-letter text

### Discussion paragraph on the bandit

Insert this as the **main Discussion paragraph** on the bandit boundary.

> A context-alert bandit provides a strong handcrafted stress test because it maps online alert proxies directly to specialist choices without privileged final-test labels. In our comparisons, this memoryless context rule remains competitive with PD-PPO, which means the paper should not claim universal superiority over all context-aware heuristics. At the same time, the bandit does not replace the method claim pursued here: PD-PPO is a sequential constrained scheduling framework trained from forecast loss under executable-mask dynamics. The held-out results therefore support a narrower and stronger conclusion: prediction-driven masked RL consistently improves over fixed, simple dynamic, and myopic forecast-greedy references, while a handcrafted context-aware rule remains an important boundary baseline.

This wording is reviewer-safe because it is honest about the boundary while still protecting the framework claim. It also fits the manuscript’s existing logic around fixed-mask replay, privileged diagnostics, and bounded claims under a fixed evaluation forecaster. fileciteturn0file0

> We performed a bounded development study to test whether clean context-aware architectural changes could improve PD-PPO against the context-alert bandit without changing the method’s core identity.
